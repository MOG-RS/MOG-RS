MOGAD: Understanding Myelin Oligodendrocyte Glycoprotein Antibody-Associated Disease

A clear and practical guide for patients and families

What is MOGAD?

MOG = Myelin Oligodendrocyte Glycoprotein

  • MOG protects nerves and helps transmit nerve signals.
  • In MOGAD, the immune system attacks MOG-IgG, causing inflammation in the optic nerves, spinal cord, and/or brain.

Who is Affected?

  • Can affect both children and adults.
  • It is neither inherited nor contagious.
  • Sometimes occurs after infections, but the cause is not always known.

MOGAD Symptoms

👁️

Optic Neuritis

Vision loss, eye pain, blurred vision, or muted colors.

⚡

Myelitis

Weakness in arms or legs, tingling, bladder or bowel problems.

🧠

Encephalitis / ADEM

Confusion, headache, sleepiness, seizures.

Similarities and Differences with MS and NMOSD

Similarities:
Affect myelin, cause inflammatory attacks, require specialist care.
Cause:
MOGAD = MOG antibodies
NMOSD = AQP4 antibodies
MS = No specific antibody identified
Course:
Recovery is generally more complete in MOGAD; MS is often chronic.
Treatment & Prognosis:
30–60% of MOGAD patients have a single episode (monophasic).

Diagnosis

🩸

Blood Test

Detect MOG-IgG antibodies

🔬

MRI

Identify areas of inflammation

💉

Lumbar Puncture

If indicated

👨‍⚕️

Clinical Evaluation

By a neurologist specialized in neuroimmunology

Treatment

During Attacks:

Intravenous corticosteroids; if needed, plasma exchange (PLEX) or IV immunoglobulins (IVIG).

Relapse Prevention:

Immunosuppressants, oral corticosteroids, or monthly IVIG depending on the case.

Note: Not all patients require long-term preventive treatment.

Prognosis

  • Recovery after attacks is usually good.
  • 30–60% of patients experience a monophasic course (single episode).
  • Persistent symptoms (fatigue, pain, visual or urinary issues) can be managed with a multidisciplinary care team.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does MOGAD last forever?

Many people experience only a single episode, and not everyone will have additional attacks.

Can I exercise?

Yes. Physical activity is safe and beneficial when adapted to your abilities and condition.

Is pregnancy safe?

Generally yes. Most pregnancies proceed normally with careful monitoring by your healthcare team.

MOGAD can feel overwhelming at first, but with accurate diagnosis, proper treatment, and follow-up, most people regain a satisfactory level of functioning. Being informed and actively involved in your care makes a difference.
